"I ate really healthy today — only half a bowl of rice, plus sweet potato and corn." It sounds fine, but it hides a common misconception: treating sweet potato, corn and potato as "vegetables" added on top of the meal. They are good foods, but they're high in starch and, nutritionally, closer to staples than to leafy greens.

For people managing weight or blood sugar, the key is putting them in the right slot: they should **replace** part of your rice, noodles or steamed buns — not be **added** as an extra serving alongside your usual staples.

## They're here to replace staples, not to add on

If a meal includes sweet potato, corn or potato, cut the rice, noodles, flatbread or buns accordingly. Otherwise you're eating a double serving of staple, and energy and carbs easily run over.

Cooking method matters too: steaming, boiling and roasting keep fat in check; fries, chips, candied sweet potato and oil-braised potato pack in extra oil, sugar and energy — and can't be called a healthy choice just because "the raw material is potato."

## Don't treat everything "grown in the ground" as a vegetable

"Vegetable" isn't one uniform category. Nutritionally it splits roughly into two:

- **Non-starchy vegetables**: leafy greens, gourds and eggplants, mushrooms, kelp and the like — high in water, low in energy, mainly providing fiber, vitamins and minerals. These are the "half plate" every meal should have.
- **Starchy vegetables**: sweet potato, corn, potato, yam, taro, pumpkin and so on — high in starch, closer to staple substitutes.

So "I had an ear of corn" doesn't count as "vegetables done." Every meal still needs non-starchy vegetables, while the starchy serving is deducted from your staple.

## FAQ

### Should I just avoid sweet potato and corn, then?

No. They're rich in fiber, potassium and some vitamins, and make a fine part of your staples — the key is to substitute, portion, and cook with little oil.

### Is potato a vegetable or a staple?

By starch and energy, potato is closer to a staple. Eating a lot of it as a "side dish" while still having a full serving of rice easily pushes carbs over.

## Summary

Put sweet potato, corn and potato back in the "staple substitute" slot, and you add variety and fiber while keeping a clear sense of how much staple a meal holds. Remember one line: they're here to replace staples, not to be an extra course.
