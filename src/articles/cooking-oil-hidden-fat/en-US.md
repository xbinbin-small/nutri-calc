"The doctor said my lipids are high, eat less oil." So someone halves the oil in their stir-fries — the food turns bland, they don't lose weight, and the lipids still don't drop. The problem is usually this: they fixate on the bottle of oil at home but miss the "invisible oil" in fried chicken, flaky pastries, milk tea and takeout.

Fat itself isn't the enemy — it's part of cell membranes, helps absorb vitamins A, D, E and K, and makes food tastier and more filling. Healthy eating is never "not a drop of oil"; it's controlling the total amount, improving the type, and especially hunting down the hidden fat you eat without noticing.

## The bottom line first

- Fat is an essential nutrient; cutting oil entirely is neither realistic nor necessary.
- China's dietary guidelines suggest adults keep cooking oil to 25–30 g per day.
- "Visible oil" is easy to control; what really gets out of hand is the "hidden fat" in processed foods and eating out.
- Changing the cooking method first (more steaming/boiling/stewing, less deep-frying) usually beats agonizing over which oil to buy.
- You can rotate vegetable oils, but even the best oil, over the limit, still adds weight and raises lipids.

## Visible oil vs invisible oil

The oil you pour in when stir-frying, dressing or frying is "visible oil" — relatively easy to estimate and control. The tricky part is "invisible oil," hidden in:

- Fried chicken, fried skewers, fries, fried dough sticks;
- Flaky pastries, shortbread, croissant-type bread, all kinds of biscuits;
- Cream cakes, cream toppings, cheese, butter;
- Processed meats such as sausage, bacon and luncheon meat;
- Hot-pot dipping sauces, salad dressing, rich broths and oily takeout.

If you're worried about weight, lipids or fatty liver, cutting oil from these first usually helps more than scraping off one more spoonful from your home stir-fry.

## Control oil by starting with the method

Instead of agonizing over which oil to buy, adjust the cooking method first:

- Use more steaming, boiling, stewing, braising and quick stir-frying; less deep-frying, dry-frying and repeated frying.
- Use a graduated oil bottle or measuring spoon — don't pour "by feel."
- Trim fat and skin from meat, and buy less processed meat.
- For takeout, choose combinations with vegetables and protein, less deep-frying and less rich broth.
- Rotate vegetable oils, but still keep the daily total within guideline limits.

## Summary

Less oil doesn't mean bland — it means the food's own flavor and the meal's energy are easier to manage. What really needs cutting is usually not the bottle at home, but the hidden fat in fried food, pastries and takeout. Change the method first, then control the total — far more practical than just "switching oils."
