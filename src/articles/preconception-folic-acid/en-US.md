Many people only think "should I start folic acid?" after two lines appear on a pregnancy test. But the fetal neural tube begins to close in the first few weeks of pregnancy — when many women don't yet know they are pregnant. Starting only after you find out may already miss the key window. So with folic acid, the emphasis is on the word "preconception."

Folate is a B vitamin involved in cell division and DNA synthesis. Women trying to conceive take folic acid regularly mainly to lower the risk of fetal neural tube defects (such as spina bifida and anencephaly). This article is for women who are trying to conceive or planning to, and the family who care about it.

## The bottom line first

- Women planning a pregnancy are generally advised to start regular folic acid at least 3 months beforehand.
- The usual recommended dose is 0.4 mg (400 µg) per day, continuing through the end of the first trimester.
- Those with a prior neural-tube-defect pregnancy, certain medications, or absorption disorders may need a different dose — follow medical advice.
- Food folate is easily lost during storage and cooking, so supplementation is still advised while trying to conceive.
- Folic acid does not replace regular prenatal care, and it isn't "take it and you're set."

## Why start three months ahead

The neural tube largely closes around 3–4 weeks after conception, and many people are already past that point when they confirm the pregnancy. Starting a few months ahead keeps body folate at a steadier level, covering this most critical — and most easily missed — early developmental period.

For women planning a pregnancy, the usual advice is to start at least 3 months beforehand, taking 0.4 mg of folic acid daily and continuing through the end of the first trimester (in some cases through the whole pregnancy, per your doctor). If you have had a neural-tube-defect pregnancy, take certain medications such as some antiepileptic drugs, or have a folate absorption disorder, the required dose may differ markedly — have a doctor decide, and don't increase it on your own.

## Can food alone provide enough?

Dark green vegetables, beans, animal liver, whole grains and some fruit all contain folate, and of course you should eat more of them while trying to conceive. But natural food folate is not very stable — heat, light and long storage all reduce it — so diet alone often can't reliably reach the level needed before pregnancy. "Regular supplementation plus a balanced diet" is the safer combination, not one or the other.

And remember, preconception nutrition is not only folic acid. Protein, iron, iodine, vitamin D and overall diet quality all matter; so do weight, sleep, and quitting smoking and limiting alcohol.

## FAQ

### Do men need folic acid?

The standard recommendation is mainly for women trying to conceive and during pregnancy. Men can simply keep a balanced diet; ask a doctor about any extra supplementation rather than following along blindly.

### My multivitamin contains folate — do I still need a separate dose?

It depends on whether it provides 0.4 mg and whether you're stacking other products. If unsure, show what you're taking to a doctor or pharmacist to avoid getting too much or too little.

## Summary

The core of preconception folic acid is: start at least 3 months ahead, 0.4 mg a day, taken regularly, alongside a balanced diet and regular prenatal care. Adjust the dose under medical advice in high-risk situations. Preparing before pregnancy means far more than scrambling to catch up afterward.
