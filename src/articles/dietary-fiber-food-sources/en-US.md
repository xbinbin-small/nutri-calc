Constipated and wanting to "clean out the gut," many people add a plate of greens at dinner. A few days later nothing has changed, so they conclude "eating more vegetables doesn't help either." The problem usually isn't whether you ate vegetables — it's that your fiber sources are too narrow; a single plate of greens is far from enough.

Dietary fiber comes from several food groups, and a chronic shortfall is often tied to overly refined staples, too few beans, and a monotonous choice of fruit and vegetables. This article makes clear where fiber actually comes from and how to add it steadily.

## The bottom line first

- Fiber doesn't come only from vegetables; whole grains, mixed beans, fruit, nuts and seeds matter just as much.
- China's dietary guidelines suggest adults aim for about 25–30 g of fiber per day.
- Swapping some refined rice and flour for whole grains and mixed beans usually beats just eating one more plate of vegetables.
- Increase fiber gradually and with plenty of water, or you may get bloated.
- Fiber isn't medicine; it brings your diet from "refined and monotonous" back to "whole and varied."

## Fiber sources beyond vegetables

Many assume "fiber = eating vegetables," but it's spread across several food groups:

- **Whole grains**: oats, brown rice, whole wheat, corn, buckwheat, etc.;
- **Mixed beans**: red beans, mung beans, chickpeas, kidney beans, etc.;
- **Vegetables**: especially leafy greens, mushrooms, gourds and eggplants;
- **Fruit**: whole fruit beats juice, which loses most of the fiber;
- **Nuts and seeds**: small amounts are enough — watch the energy.

The most effective yet most overlooked source is your staple. Swap part of your daily white rice and flour for oats, brown rice, corn or mixed-bean rice, and fiber rises steadily — easier to sustain than just "adding another plate of vegetables."

## Adding fiber: gradually, and don't forget water

Fiber is good, but adding it too fast causes trouble. Suddenly eating a lot of coarse grains or beans may bring bloating, more gas, even diarrhea. A safer way:

- Start with a small daily serving of whole grains or mixed beans; let your gut adjust for a few days before adding more;
- Ensure adequate water — fiber needs water to work well;
- Pair with regular activity, which also helps bowel movement.

People in an active phase of gastrointestinal disease, with swallowing difficulty, or on special therapeutic diets should consult a professional before increasing fiber.

## A day's worth of fiber (example)

- Breakfast: oatmeal or whole-wheat bread + a serving of fruit;
- Lunch: mixed-grain rice instead of white rice + two kinds of vegetables + a serving of soy products;
- Dinner: sweet potato or corn replacing part of the staple + leafy greens;
- Snack: a small amount of plain nuts or whole fruit.

## Summary

To get enough fiber, don't fixate on that plate of greens at dinner. Make your staple "whole," vary your beans, fruit and vegetables, add gradually and drink plenty of water — and fiber takes care of itself. It's not a drug for constipation, but it makes your overall diet structure healthier.
