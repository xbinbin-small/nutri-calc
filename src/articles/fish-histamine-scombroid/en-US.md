Soon after a fish meal, you suddenly flush, get a headache, palpitations and itchy skin — many people's first thought is "I'm allergic to seafood." But if you've eaten the same fish before with no problem and only reacted this time, the issue may not be allergy but the fish itself — this is a histamine-related food poisoning.

In some fish stored improperly, bacteria convert the histidine in the flesh into histamine. Once histamine reaches a certain level, eating it triggers allergy-like reactions. The culprit isn't your constitution, but the fish having been out of cold storage too long.

## Not an allergy — histamine at work

A true food allergy is the immune system reacting to specific food proteins, and it usually flares every time you eat that food. Histamine poisoning, however, is from too much histamine accumulating in the fish, and has little to do with personal constitution — people who shared the same batch of fish may all develop flushing, headache, palpitations and itchy, reddened skin. That's one key difference from allergy.

## The key is the cold chain, not cooking time

Dark-fleshed fish such as tuna, mackerel, sardines and saury are more prone to this. The key points:

- The longer a fish is out of cold storage (refrigeration/freezing), the more active the bacteria and the more histamine accumulates;
- **Once histamine forms, ordinary frying, steaming or boiling can't remove it** — so "cooked" doesn't mean safe;
- Therefore preventing histamine relies on a full cold chain and freshness, not on a few more minutes of cooking.

Practical tips: choose fresh fish from reliable sources with an intact cold chain; don't buy or eat fish with a clear off smell or abnormal color or texture; refrigerate or cook it soon after buying, and don't leave it at room temperature for long.

## When symptoms are clear, treat it as an emergency

If you feel unwell after eating fish — especially if several people who shared the same batch react together, or there's difficulty breathing, marked palpitations, persistent vomiting or altered consciousness — seek care immediately, don't tough it out or "eat a bit more to test." Tell the doctor when you ate and what kind of food it was, and keep leftover food or packaging to help identify the cause.

## Summary

An "allergy-like" reaction after eating fish may not be a real allergy — it's often histamine poisoning from poor storage. Fish is highly nutritious and worth eating often, but only with freshness and a full cold chain. Remember: heating can't remove histamine; what matters is buying fresh, keeping it cold, and eating it promptly.
